The gap isn't the tools. It's how people use them.

Most companies have bought access to AI. Far fewer have changed how any actual work gets done.

What usually happens is that a handful of enthusiasts get fluent on their own, while most people paste in a question and take the first answer. Sometimes, without realising it, people also share information that should have stayed inside the company. The tools have arrived, and the next step is building the judgement to use them well.

Vertura's work has always been about teaching people to think with AI rather than defer to it, and we now bring the same method from students to working teams. Same principle underneath: minimum theory, maximum hands-on. Nobody sits through a slide deck about the history of large language models.

What this is not
This isn't a tool demo, a webinar, a certification course or a coding class. Nobody writes code, and nobody needs to.
What people leave with
Work they produced during the session, on their own tasks, that they can use the next morning.

Six things a team leaves able to do.

The five AI capabilities from Vertura AI Labs, translated into the work your team actually does, plus a sixth that keeps your company's data safe.

01

Think with AI

Asking better questions, prompting deliberately, and knowing when the answer on screen is wrong. The single highest-leverage habit, and the one almost nobody is taught.

02

Research with AI

Comparing sources, testing claims, and pulling a reliable summary out of a large amount of material, instead of accepting the first confident paragraph.

03

Create with AI

Drafts, decks, briefs, first-pass copy and visuals produced in minutes rather than afternoons, then edited by the person who owns the work.

04

Build with AI

Combining tools into something that runs repeatedly, such as a workflow, a template or a small internal utility the team keeps using after the session ends.

05

Solve with AI

Taking a real bottleneck from your own operation, breaking it down, and working it through with AI in the room.

06

Judge the output

Where AI is confidently wrong, what must never be pasted into it, and which decisions stay with a human. This is the part that keeps everything else safe.

How an engagement runs.

  1. 1

    A scoping call

    Which teams, what they spend their week doing, and where the time actually goes. We ask for two or three real tasks rather than hypotheticals, because those become the material.

  2. 2

    The session is built around your work

    Your documents, your processes, your bottlenecks. A finance team and a sales team leave with different outputs because they came in with different problems.

  3. 3

    The team works, live

    Everyone is on their own laptop doing their own task. We measure the session by what each person produced in it, so everyone leaves with something useful.

  4. 4

    You keep what was built

    The prompts, workflows and templates the team created stay with you, written down, so a new joiner in three months isn't starting from nothing.

Companies in non-technical industries, such as manufacturing, retail, logistics, real estate, healthcare administration, hospitality, professional services and education.
Typical audience
Whole departments rather than a nominated few. The biggest gains come when every member of the team improves, not just the enthusiasts.

Questions companies ask first.

Do our people need any technical background?

None. The training is built for people who have never written a line of code and never will. If someone can use email and a spreadsheet, they can do this.

Which AI tools do you teach?

The ones your company already pays for, wherever possible. The habits transfer between tools, like how to frame a question, how to check an answer and when to stop trusting it, and those outlast whichever product you are on this year.

What about our confidential data?

It is part of the training rather than a disclaimer at the end. Teams work through what should never be pasted into a public tool, what your own policy allows, and how to get the benefit without putting client or employee information somewhere it cannot be retrieved from.

How long does it take?

That depends on how many teams and how deep you want to go. We agree the shape together on the scoping call, so the plan fits your work rather than a fixed package.

How do we know it worked?

Ask your team which tasks they no longer do manually. The session produces real outputs on real tasks, so the evidence is in the work rather than in a feedback form.
