Founder

Sneh Shah.

Founder of The Vertura Academy. He built the whole thing, including the school track, the Foundation Programme and AI Labs, around one idea he kept running into at work: the hard part was never the technical skill.

Schools teach students what to think. I built The Vertura Academy to teach students how to think. It is a complement to the academic foundation school already gives them, not a replacement for it.

What he saw before he started.

Sneh finished three internships before he was 22: one in data science, one in AI and data science, and one as a business analyst. Different companies, different work, and the same pattern in all three.

The technical skill was the part anyone could pick up. It was sitting free online, and the people around him had picked it up that way. What actually separated one person from another was whether they could reason from first principles instead of copying a template, hold a room when they had no authority in it, manage themselves when nobody was checking, and finish things.

Nobody had been taught any of that, not in school, not at university and not on the job. It was assumed you either had it or you didn't.

Background
Three internships, in data science, AI and data science, and business analysis.
Based in
Pune, Maharashtra, working with schools in Gujarat

The offer he turned down.

He had a full-time job waiting, with a promotion guaranteed inside the first nine months. He said no. The role was a good one, but it pointed away from the work he had decided really mattered.

What he wanted to build instead was an education system that trains those practical and people skills directly, and that can change shape as the work changes. Technical curriculums keep being overtaken, and it is hard for large institutions to keep pace from the inside.

The starting point was age 10 to 14, because that is early enough for habits to still be forming and late enough for a child to argue back.

Also built
Earlier ventures in the same direction, The Pinnacle Minds and Apex Minds, both worked on mindset, thinking and decision-making with 9 to 14 year olds. Vertura is where that work came together.

How that shows up in the programmes.

Every session ends with something the student made, because he doesn't believe a child has learnt a concept they can't use or explain. Every student is measured on the Growth Index rather than handed a certificate, because he wants families to see real, specific progress. Every programme has an admission conversation, because a group works best when every student in it truly wants to be there.

He runs the admission conversations himself.
