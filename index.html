    What school leaders say.

    An academic record is no longer the whole of a child's preparation.

    Many of the jobs today's children will do don't exist yet. One widely quoted estimate, from the World Economic Forum, puts it at around 65%. Vertura was built around that idea.

    School still matters as much as ever. But knowing a subject and being able to use it in a world that keeps changing are two different abilities, and school naturally focuses on the first.

    A child can top their class and still find it hard to hold a room, structure an argument they haven't rehearsed, keep a commitment nobody is checking, or decide what to do when the syllabus runs out. These skills can be trained, but there is rarely space for them in the timetable.

    Vertura teaches them the way they are actually used: through work the student produces, defends and revises. Every session ends with something the child made and can keep.

    Every Vertura programme is built from the same six capabilities:

    The same four steps run every session, in every programme.

    A student never leaves with only notes. Each session ends in a tangible output, such as a worksheet, a recording, a plan or something they built, that goes home with them.

    1. 1

      Learn

      The concept is introduced in plain language, with an example from a situation a 10 to 16 year old will recognise.

    2. 2

      Apply

      The student uses it immediately on a live problem, working alone or against the rest of the room.

    3. 3

      Build or explain

      They produce something or teach the idea back. If they cannot explain it, they have not learnt it yet.

    4. 4

      Reflect

      They review what they did, what it cost them, and what they would change next time. That review is recorded.

    The Vertura Academy

    Teaches how to think, act and lead, with no syllabus to cover.

    Measures a child on six capabilities through the Vertura Growth Index, scored quarterly.

    Assessment is what the student built, argued for and revised.

    The output is something the child keeps, like a project, a plan or a recording.

    Foundation cohorts are capped at 20 and mentored at 20:1. In collaborating schools, a Vertura faculty member leads every section.

    The Growth Index scores six measured dimensions: cognitive growth, discipline consistency, communication development, leadership behaviour, project quality, and the mentor's own observations. It goes to parents, or to the collaborating school, each quarter, and shows real progress in a way a participation certificate can't.

    Three things we hold to, including when it costs us a seat.

    Clarity of thought

    Here, the reasoning behind an answer matters as much as the answer itself. Students are asked to show how they got there, not just the conclusion.

    Execution over ideation

    Children are full of great ideas. Here, marks come from the ideas that got finished, tested and shown to someone outside the room.

    Rise through discipline

    We reward consistent effort over talent alone. The habit systems are taught first because everything else depends on them.

    Selectivity
    Every programme begins with a genuine admission conversation rather than a simple sign-up, and cohorts close once they are full so that every student gets proper attention.

    The person behind it.

    Sneh Shah founded The Vertura Academy after turning down a full-time offer that came with a promotion guaranteed inside nine months.

    He had already done three internships, in data science, AI and data science, and business analysis, and the same thing kept showing up. The technical skill was the part you could learn free, online, in a month. What separated people was whether they could think clearly under pressure, hold a room, and finish what they started. None of that had been taught to any of them.

    So he built the thing he wished had existed at twelve: a system that trains the practical and people skills a career actually runs on, and that adapts as the work changes, rather than another programme focused only on theory and marks.

    Questions parents and principals ask first.

    Does this replace school?

    No, and it is not designed to. Vertura covers no syllabus, sets no academic homework and gives no exam coaching. It runs on weekends or online precisely so it sits alongside school. In collaborating schools it runs inside the timetable, agreed with the school, and still doesn't touch subject teaching.

    How do the three programmes relate to each other?

    They are built from the same six capabilities, run the same four-step session method and are measured by the same Growth Index. What changes is the age, the depth and the route in. The Foundation Programme is the full eighteen-month build in person. AI Labs is a shorter, online, outcome-specific lab inside one field. Vertura for Schools puts the capabilities into a school's own week for whole grades.

    Which one fits my child?

    If your child is 10 to 14 and you want the full build, the Foundation Programme. If they are 13 to 16 and the pull is AI specifically, or you want to see how they respond before committing to eighteen months, AI Labs. If you are a school leader rather than a parent, the school track. If you're unsure, just say so in the enquiry. The admission conversation is partly there to help with this.

    What does my child actually come home with?

    Something physical or recorded from every single session, such as a worksheet they completed, a recording of them speaking, a plan they wrote or a prototype they built. By the end of the Foundation Programme that includes a project built, tested with real users and presented to a jury of practising professionals.

    How is progress measured, and how will I know it's real?

    Through the Vertura Growth Index: a quarterly scored assessment across the six capabilities, based on what the student produced rather than on a test day. You get the specific movement per capability, not a participation certificate.

    How does selection work?

    You enquire, and we set up a twenty-minute conversation with the student. It is with your child rather than with you, and it is not a written exam. We ask three things: what are you good at that your report card has never measured, what do you want to be known for, and where do you feel most stuck right now? It is there to check readiness and real motivation, not ability. Seats are confirmed after that conversation, and cohorts close at 20.

    Still unsure?
    Ask the question directly in the enquiry form. It comes to us, not to a call centre.
